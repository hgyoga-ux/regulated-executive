The Method

N · L · S · L

There is a way through. It's not what you've been told. It's not what you've tried before. Four small habits. One big shift.

L

Light — Clarity

Reduce the volume of each breath. Less air, not more. Light breathing improves oxygen delivery and flow to the brain — bringing clarity of thought, focus, and presence.

S

Slow — Calm

Decelerate your respiratory rate. A slower breath stimulates the vagus nerve, which signals safety to the brain — activating calm, reducing reactivity, restoring composure.

L

Low — Command

Breathe low in the body — supported by the diaphragm. Low breathing improves your ability to handle stress and steadies attention, giving you command under pressure.

The Framework

Green — Ready

Clear thinking. Decisive. Fully present. Peak performance.

Yellow — Reacting

Activated. Necessary for growth. The skill is returning to green quickly.

Orange — Injured

Insomnia. Irritability. Decision fatigue. Burnout approaching.

Red — Ill

Complete breakdown. Chronic illness. System failure.

From Surviving to Flourishing

Because leadership should not require sacrificing your well-being.

The FOSS Approach

Flourishing · Operating · Struggling · Surviving

Every leader moves through different states of capacity. There are times when we flourish, times when we operate effectively, and times when growing demands leave us struggling or simply surviving.

These experiences are not fixed identities. They are signals that help us understand our present capacity and what may be needed. The FOSS framework provides a practical way to recognize these shifts and develop the skills to respond.

The goal is not to remain in Flourishing every moment. It is to build the awareness, flexibility, and physiological capacity to move toward Flourishing more consistently.

Flourishing is a direction we cultivate, not a standard we must constantly meet.

Two Complementary Approaches

FOSS — Building Capacity Over Time. FOSS guides the ongoing development of physiological awareness and self-regulation. Through breath reeducation and personally relevant practices, leaders explore how to sustain their energy, respond with greater clarity, and restore their capacity following demanding experiences.

COSC — Recognizing Stress Under Pressure. The Combat and Operational Stress Control (COSC) Stress Continuum offers a framework for recognizing changes in functioning under pressure and identifying when additional support or intervention may be appropriate. Originally developed for military environments, its principles offer a useful perspective on the demands faced by leaders working through sustained pressure and uncertainty.

Within The Regulated Executive, this framework informs awareness and appropriate responses to stress, while breath reeducation provides practical skills that participants can apply in their daily lives. Together, these approaches support both ongoing development and more informed responses to challenging moments.

What Flourishing Leadership Can Look Like

H.E.R. — Hardiness · Endurance · Resilience

Hardiness. Remaining grounded and able to think clearly when situations are uncertain, difficult, or changing.

Endurance. Meeting sustained responsibilities while recognizing the importance of pacing, recovery, and replenishing personal resources.

Resilience. Recognizing when capacity has been stretched, responding constructively, and restoring the ability to engage with what matters.

These qualities are not about enduring limitless pressure. They are about developing the capacity to lead with greater awareness, effectiveness, and care.
